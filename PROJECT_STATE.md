# GENESIS project state

## Objective and architecture

Build a zero-player browser ecosystem in which each visitor watches an independent, persistent world evolve under deterministic simulation. React hosts the spectator UI; PixiJS renders it. `src/simulation/worker.ts` runs `SimulationEngine` off the main thread. The engine owns terrain, climate, organisms, behavior, genetics, resources, events, and seeded PRNG state. `src/persistence/` validates and stores saves in IndexedDB with recovery; `SessionCoordinator` elects one writing tab and broadcasts snapshots to observer tabs.

## Behavior and implemented systems

`src/simulation/ecology/config.ts` defaults to **Model 0**, the production behavior. Model 1 adds scored foraging, route rejection, mate and local-food gates, and spatial plant reseeding. Model 2 changes Model 1's shared-food competition calculation. Both remain opt-in experiments; no experimental ecology parameters were promoted. Old saves lacking a behavior model resolve to Model 0; saved RNG state and optional ecology configuration support replay. The latest audit warns that older app builds may reject a Model 2 save.

Verified in source and tests: procedural terrain and seeded world generation; autonomous feeding, predation, metabolism, hydration, reproduction, genetics, climate and events; spectator camera and chronicle; save validation and recovery; leader/observer tab coordination; behavior model selection and replay tests. A code path for species formation exists, but spontaneous speciation has **not** been observed in completed benchmark sets. Multi-tab safety is implemented, though this review did not independently test every browser failure mode.

## Latest evidence and open problems

The [population regulation audit](docs/audits/POPULATION_REGULATION_AUDIT.md) ran three reused development seeds for 10,000 ticks each under Model 1 single births, Model 2 single births, and unsaved Model 1 litter v1, plus three selected 30,000-tick extensions. Eight short runs never touched the 220-organism cap; litter seed 260679 touched it for 10 ticks, with zero blocked births. A bounded isolated 260-cap copy reproduced all 40 core checkpoints for that run and still peaked at 220. Plant consumption exceeded measured solar production in eight of nine short runs; low local food, reduced mate eligibility, adult deaths, and declines occurred without cap enforcement. Model 1 and Model 2 seed 691153 showed population recovery after resource regeneration by 30,000 ticks, while litter seed 260679 went extinct at tick 16,000 despite later plant recovery. This supports meaningful resource-dependent feedback with boom-and-bust behavior, not stable regulation or a source-level defect requiring a new control mechanism. Long-run cap saturation in earlier 50,000-tick sets remains unresolved; no model was adopted or changed.

The [juvenile survival audit](docs/audits/JUVENILE_SURVIVAL_AUDIT.md) traced age-only maturity (117–132 ticks in three tested herbivore genomes) and adult-equivalent juvenile metabolism, feeding, and predation. In 45 cloned-state Model 1 trials across seeds 691153, 260679, 10101, all 36 focal juveniles without immediate predator contact matured, including nine with no food; the nine exposed at immediate predator contact died on tick 1. With no food, lower reserves caused earlier post-maturity starvation (mean death tick 1,289 for singles among two deaths, 1,052 for twins, 911 for triplets); one single was censored alive at tick 1,400. No no-food focal reproduced. The seed-260679 5,000-tick litter density trace reached 220 for 10 ticks but recorded no cap-blocked births; local food and mating eligibility then fell and adult deaths reduced population. This is a controlled development diagnostic, not a long-run density solution. No lifecycle candidate was adopted. The suite has **99 passing tests**; lint and build pass.

The [predator scoring validation](docs/audits/PREDATOR_SCORING_VALIDATION.md) branched 16 rejected-prey cases from cloned Model 1 development states (seeds 691153, 260679, 10101; 120 ticks per branch). Forced pursuit killed five selected prey but improved incremental net energy in only four branches; aggregate incremental net was negative in both rejection categories. **No scoring defect was confirmed, and no scoring model was added.** The [reproductive strategy experiment](docs/audits/REPRODUCTIVE_STRATEGY_EXPERIMENT.md) paired single births against an unsaved litter development switch on the same three seeds for 5,000 ticks. Litter runs had 538 mating events, 681 offspring, 680 reaching maturity by the horizon, and zero final predators, versus 605 events, 605 offspring, 599 reaching maturity, and zero final predators with single births. One litter run touched the population cap for 10 ticks. Reproduction charges, newborn reserves, and overhead reconciled exactly; the litter rule was **not adopted**. These reused development seeds are not an independent holdout.

The latest completed predator energy audit reports **97 passing tests in two files**, TypeScript lint and build passing. A focused post-birth save/restore test replayed identically for 20 ticks. The earlier performance audit reported 95 tests. [Audit evidence](docs/audits/SIMULATION_PERFORMANCE_AUDIT.md) reports identical six saved-state hashes for Models 0/1 across three seeds after optimization and matching ecological checkpoints for 20 seeds through 50,000 ticks. Model 1's 20-seed 50,000-tick runtime sum fell from 843.977 to 758.567 seconds; Model 0 had no demonstrated speed change. These are headless measurements, not browser frame rates.

The same audit found no carnivores at 10,000 or 50,000 ticks in the compared development seeds. Model 1's 11 surviving worlds in the earlier 30-seed 50,000-tick run had all touched the 220-organism cap. Model 2 reduced cap occupancy from 36,640 to 19,252 ticks across 20 development seeds at 50,000 ticks; the new short-run cap comparison does not resolve that longer-run occupancy or establish cap dependence. [Predator viability diagnostics](docs/audits/PREDATOR_VIABILITY.md) used three paired development seeds for 5,000 ticks under Models 0–2 and six one-factor Model 1/2 interventions. Natural Model 1 predator food gain was 3,384 versus 5,353 energy spent, with no births. A 1.3× founder predator food-processing intervention produced five mature offspring, three later-generation births, and four surviving descendants, all in one seed; the other two seeds and all Model 2 runs still lost every predator. Energy and mate access remain joint constraints. [Predator energy audit](docs/audits/PREDATOR_ENERGY_AUDIT.md) confirms that the reproduction tax used an estimated child reserve rather than the actual newborn reserve; successful births now book actual overhead, and failed queued births refund parent charges. Three Model 1 development seeds recorded 78 hunt episodes, 55 kills, 56 nonlethal strikes, and zero births. Seed 260679 had reachable prey often but only 10 accepted hunt episodes; candidate checks recorded 5,727 too-fast and 24,073 negative-value rejections. A reserve cap withheld 90 energy units across all 55 kills, too little to explain the predator deficit. No production ecology behavior changed. Poor long-run survival and unproven population-level speciation remain open.

## Decisions, phase, and next task

The [predator capture experiment](docs/audits/PREDATOR_CAPTURE_EXPERIMENT.md) tested one unsaved Model 1 wounded-prey follow-through rule from 22 matched, naturally occurring post-strike encounters on seeds 691153, 260679, 10101. Baseline and candidate each captured all 22 targets, made 26 strikes, and returned identical 1,381.263 net focal-predator energy over the bounded branches. The rule made no measured improvement and is **rejected**; no 5,000-tick candidate population screen or independent holdout was warranted. Earlier ordinary Model 1 runs still had zero predator births and zero predators at tick 5,000 in all three seeds. Reproductive replacement remains unproven. The development-only probe does not alter default Model 0, ordinary Model 1, or Model 2. Future predator work needs stage-specific evidence of lost, profitable accepted hunts; do not retune this follow-through rule on these seeds.

Keep Model 0 in production: Model 1 failed predator viability and earlier long-run cap goals; Model 2 improved one cap measure but did not solve either problem. The [integrated ecosystem architecture review](docs/ECOSYSTEM_ARCHITECTURE_REVIEW.md) prioritized joint predator/prey/mate encounter placement, shared renewable-food competition, and condition-dependent juvenile and birth investment. The first [isolated placement screen](docs/audits/PREDATOR_PLACEMENT_EXPERIMENT.md) is complete on three reused development seeds at 5,000 ticks. It relocated only nine Model 1 predator founders through a separate generation RNG. Mate access rose, but predator net energy remained negative in all seeds; three offspring matured, none reproduced or survived to 5,000, and there were no generation-3+ births. **The placement gate failed; do not retune on these seeds or promote it.** Production Model 0 and Model 2 remain unchanged. Actual post-birth saves replayed identically, and 106 tests, TypeScript check, and build passed at that stage. The [determinism baseline audit](docs/audits/DETERMINISM_BASELINE_AUDIT.md) traced the six older 2,000-tick hash mismatches to the intentional reproduction-tax ledger correction in `054e9e1`: all six archived hashes reproduce at `50208b1`, and that ledger is the sole differing saved field at the same horizons. A new versioned baseline covers Models 0–2 on seeds 10101, 691153, 260679; fresh repeats and tick-1,000 save/restore match through tick 2,000. This is a reproducibility gate, not ecological validation. The shared-food and juvenile candidates were subsequently tested and rejected. Spontaneous population-level speciation remains unproven and deferred.

Detailed evidence: [audit index](docs/audits/README.md), especially [predator energy](docs/audits/PREDATOR_ENERGY_AUDIT.md), [predator viability](docs/audits/PREDATOR_VIABILITY.md), [performance and Model 2](docs/audits/SIMULATION_PERFORMANCE_AUDIT.md), [behavior repair](docs/audits/ECOLOGY_BEHAVIOR_REPAIR.md), [calibration](docs/audits/ECOLOGY_CALIBRATION.md), [diagnostics](docs/audits/ECOLOGY_DIAGNOSTICS.md), and [historical baseline](docs/audits/SIMULATION_AUDIT.md). Raw JSON and profile outputs are in `docs/audits/data/`; consult only the result relevant to the task.

The [shared renewable-food experiment](docs/audits/RENEWABLE_FOOD_FEEDBACK_EXPERIMENT.md) tested one unsaved Model 2 mating estimator on five controlled layouts and paired seeds 691153, 260679, 10101, and 166064 through 10,000 ticks. It combined reachable plant stock with projected current growth over the parental cooldown, shared among nearby plant eaters, plus separate scavenger carrion. Controlled layouts passed and runtime stayed within the predeclared 1.5× bound. But seed 166064 reached the 220 cap for 594 ticks with 2,221 blocked mating attempts, versus zero in ordinary Model 2; scavengers still dominated, all predators disappeared, and seed 260679 went extinct in both conditions. **Reject the candidate; do not retune on these seeds.** The option remains development-only and unsaved. The unchanged version-2 baseline, candidate repeat/save continuation, 112 tests, TypeScript, and build passed at that stage.

The [juvenile investment experiment](docs/audits/JUVENILE_INVESTMENT_EXPERIMENT.md) completed the third architecture priority on reused seeds 691153, 260679, 10101. An unsaved Model 1 development gate required genetic minimum age, eight actual juvenile food-energy units, reserve at least 30, and health at least 70. Across 135 cloned 1,400-tick branches, ordinary single birth, developmental single birth, and litter v1 were compared under abundant/moderate/no food, competition, and natural predator pursuit. The gate prevented nine no-food maturity labels but changed no fed focal death or successful first-reproduction time; no-food ordinary juveniles already had zero offspring. Five no-food death times differed under age-dependent mate provisioning, but eight died in each condition. All fed focal branches matured at their ordinary genetic age. Litter v1 increased abundant-food focal offspring from 21 to 34 across nine branches, without establishing a scarcity or lifetime fitness gain. **Reject the developmental candidate.** The combined treatment and paired 5,000-tick population screen were not justified or run. Model 0 production and Models 1–2 remain unchanged. Same-seed repeat and tick-300 save continuation matched through tick 600 only when the unsaved intake sidecar was restored. The unchanged version-2 baseline passed; 114 tests, TypeScript, and build passed. The architecture roadmap is closed: placement, renewable-food feedback, and this juvenile gate all failed their respective advancement gates. The next engineering phase should plan a coordinated redesign of predator income/mate access, shared renewable food flow, and paid offspring investment with persistent replay-safe lifecycle state, rather than another isolated parameter change on these seeds.
