# GENESIS project state

## Objective and architecture

Build a zero-player browser ecosystem in which each visitor watches an independent, persistent world evolve under deterministic simulation. React hosts the spectator UI; PixiJS renders it. `src/simulation/worker.ts` runs `SimulationEngine` off the main thread. The engine owns terrain, climate, organisms, behavior, genetics, resources, events, and seeded PRNG state. `src/persistence/` validates and stores saves in IndexedDB with recovery; `SessionCoordinator` elects one writing tab and broadcasts snapshots to observer tabs.

## Behavior and implemented systems

`src/simulation/ecology/config.ts` defaults to **Model 0**, the production behavior. Model 1 adds scored foraging, route rejection, mate and local-food gates, and spatial plant reseeding. Model 2 changes Model 1's shared-food competition calculation. Both remain opt-in experiments; no experimental ecology parameters were promoted. Old saves lacking a behavior model resolve to Model 0; saved RNG state and optional ecology configuration support replay. The latest audit warns that older app builds may reject a Model 2 save.

Verified in source and tests: procedural terrain and seeded world generation; autonomous feeding, predation, metabolism, hydration, reproduction, genetics, climate and events; spectator camera and chronicle; save validation and recovery; leader/observer tab coordination; behavior model selection and replay tests. A code path for species formation exists, but spontaneous speciation has **not** been observed in completed benchmark sets. Multi-tab safety is implemented, though this review did not independently test every browser failure mode.

## Latest evidence and open problems

The [predator scoring validation](docs/audits/PREDATOR_SCORING_VALIDATION.md) branched 16 rejected-prey cases from cloned Model 1 development states (seeds 691153, 260679, 10101; 120 ticks per branch). Forced pursuit killed five selected prey but improved incremental net energy in only four branches; aggregate incremental net was negative in both rejection categories. **No scoring defect was confirmed, and no scoring model was added.** The [reproductive strategy experiment](docs/audits/REPRODUCTIVE_STRATEGY_EXPERIMENT.md) paired single births against an unsaved litter development switch on the same three seeds for 5,000 ticks. Litter runs had 538 mating events, 681 offspring, 680 reaching maturity by the horizon, and zero final predators, versus 605 events, 605 offspring, 599 reaching maturity, and zero final predators with single births. One litter run touched the population cap for 10 ticks. Reproduction charges, newborn reserves, and overhead reconciled exactly; the litter rule was **not adopted**. These reused development seeds are not an independent holdout. The current full suite has **99 passing tests**; TypeScript lint and build pass.

The latest completed predator energy audit reports **97 passing tests in two files**, TypeScript lint and build passing. A focused post-birth save/restore test replayed identically for 20 ticks. The earlier performance audit reported 95 tests. [Audit evidence](docs/audits/SIMULATION_PERFORMANCE_AUDIT.md) reports identical six saved-state hashes for Models 0/1 across three seeds after optimization and matching ecological checkpoints for 20 seeds through 50,000 ticks. Model 1's 20-seed 50,000-tick runtime sum fell from 843.977 to 758.567 seconds; Model 0 had no demonstrated speed change. These are headless measurements, not browser frame rates.

The same audit found no carnivores at 10,000 or 50,000 ticks in the compared development seeds. Model 1's 11 surviving worlds in the earlier 30-seed 50,000-tick run had all touched the 220-organism cap. Model 2 reduced cap occupancy from 36,640 to 19,252 ticks across 20 development seeds at 50,000 ticks, but still relied on the cap and had no independent holdout. [Predator viability diagnostics](docs/audits/PREDATOR_VIABILITY.md) used three paired development seeds for 5,000 ticks under Models 0–2 and six one-factor Model 1/2 interventions. Natural Model 1 predator food gain was 3,384 versus 5,353 energy spent, with no births. A 1.3× founder predator food-processing intervention produced five mature offspring, three later-generation births, and four surviving descendants, all in one seed; the other two seeds and all Model 2 runs still lost every predator. Energy and mate access remain joint constraints. [Predator energy audit](docs/audits/PREDATOR_ENERGY_AUDIT.md) confirms that the reproduction tax used an estimated child reserve rather than the actual newborn reserve; successful births now book actual overhead, and failed queued births refund parent charges. Three Model 1 development seeds recorded 78 hunt episodes, 55 kills, 56 nonlethal strikes, and zero births. Seed 260679 had reachable prey often but only 10 accepted hunt episodes; candidate checks recorded 5,727 too-fast and 24,073 negative-value rejections. A reserve cap withheld 90 energy units across all 55 kills, too little to explain the predator deficit. No production ecology behavior changed. Poor long-run survival and unproven population-level speciation remain open.

## Decisions, phase, and next task

Keep Model 0 in production: Model 1 failed predator viability and cap goals; Model 2 improved one cap measure but did not solve either problem. Preserve existing saves and deterministic trajectories while investigating mechanisms. The current phase is **after the rejected-prey and litter development screens, with no ecological candidate adopted**. Next, isolate why low-reserve juveniles mature so reliably and why predator mating remains rare. A scoring change needs evidence that it selects profitable rejections without adding costly pursuits. Do not expand to a holdout until a bounded candidate improves lineage replacement, cap occupancy, runtime, and replay in paired development seeds.
