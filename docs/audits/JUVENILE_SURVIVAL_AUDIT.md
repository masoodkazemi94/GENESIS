# Juvenile survival and density audit

## Lifecycle trace

`createOrganism` sets offspring age to zero, reserve to `round(34 + 44 × reproductiveInvestment)` for ordinary births, hydration to 85, and health to 100. The unsaved litter v1 switch divides one newborn reserve budget among siblings. Maturity occurs at `round(120 × (0.8 × genome.size + 0.2))` ticks. `updateOrganismMetabolism` flips `isMature` when age reaches that threshold, without an energy, health, feeding, or growth condition. In the three controlled genomes below, the thresholds were 132, 122, and 117 ticks.

Juveniles have the same basal, movement, temperature, flood, and hydration costs as adults; there is no juvenile cost multiplier or smaller juvenile body. Movement cost follows velocity, speed, and body size; resting multiplies total energy expenditure by 0.4. Juveniles use the same plant, carrion, or prey feeding decisions and assimilation as adults. There is no minimum reserve to remain alive before maturity: zero energy removes 0.55 health per tick, allowing a period of starvation before death. Low reserves increase food seeking but can also trigger rest below 22 energy. Predators can target juveniles under the same diet, size, camouflage, and hunt rules used for adult prey; prey age is not a defense. Reproduction requires maturity, cooldown zero, sufficient reserve and hydration, and a mate. Models 1/2 add local food per consumer gates; Model 0 does not.

## Controlled offspring trials

`npx tsx scripts/juvenile_survival_experiments.ts` generated [raw data](data/juvenile_survival_experiments.json). For each of seeds **691153, 260679, 10101**, a serialized initial Model 1 world was cloned for each condition. The same herbivore genome, coordinates, climate, terrain, and initial PRNG state were used within each single (58 energy), twin (29), and triplet (20) comparison. Each branch began with one age-zero focal organism and controlled plant and water patches. High food used six full patches; moderate food one 25-unit patch; low food no plants; competition one 65-unit patch plus 12 nonmating herbivores; predator exposure one 65-unit patch plus a nearby hungry carnivore. Spontaneously reseeded plants were removed before the next step. A compatible adult mate was placed beside a surviving focal after maturity so the 1,400-tick window could observe reproduction. These are interventions, not natural population estimates. Climate and movement still run normally; branch trajectories can diverge after different feeding decisions.

| Treatment | Single mature / 3 | Twin mature / 3 | Triplet mature / 3 | Observed adult offspring, single / twin / triplet |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| High food | 3 | 3 | 3 | 8 / 5 / 8 |
| Moderate food | 3 | 3 | 3 | 0 / 0 / 0 |
| No food | 3 | 3 | 3 | 0 / 0 / 0 |
| Local competition | 3 | 3 | 3 | 0 / 0 / 0 |
| Immediate predator exposure | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 / 0 / 0 |

Maturity ticks exactly matched each genome's age threshold in every surviving branch. Under no food, focal plant consumption was zero. Mean energy at maturity was **45.51 / 20.47 / 16.59** for single / twin / triplet starts. All no-food offspring crossed the maturity age, then starvation killed **2/3 singles, 3/3 twins, and 3/3 triplets** by tick 1,400. Mean recorded death tick among deaths was **1,289 / 1,052 / 911** respectively; one single was still alive and right-censored at 1,400. No-food adults produced no offspring. Lower reserves therefore had a genuine survival disadvantage *after* the age-based maturity event, but none in survival to that event for these three genomes. The nearby predator killed all nine focal juveniles on tick 1. This establishes susceptibility under immediate contact, not a reserve-specific predation effect.

The JSON retains each focal's exact maturity tick, energy at maturity, plant biomass consumed by maturity and through observation, energy gained, feeding events, death cause, last alive tick, adult offspring, and censoring status. High and moderate food and competition branches still had survivors at 1,400, so their offspring counts are observed-to-date, **not lifetime totals**. All completed death outcomes have zero offspring in the no-food and predator treatments. Small seed count, one focal genome per seed, imposed food layouts, and immediate predator placement limit generalization. The earlier 5,000-tick litter screen also counted births near its cutoff as nonmature; this audit does not recast those censored records as completed outcomes.

## Density feedback trace

The engine has a hard 220-organism safety ceiling. Model 1 mate decisions require local food per same-diet consumer of at least 16 (or a high-energy carnivore exception); Model 2 uses shared plant consumers for its food denominator. Litter v1 adds parent reserve, health, hydration, local food per consumer, and available-slot requirements for extra children. Plant biomass regenerates from light, temperature, moisture, fertility, and current patch condition; drought, frost, and flood suppress growth. Starvation and other deaths are processed each tick. The cap checks reject or refund births if no slot remains.

The raw JSON samples seed **260679**, Model 1 single births versus the same seed with only unsaved litter v1 enabled, for 5,000 ticks. Each 100-tick window records population, juvenile count, plant biomass, mean local plant biomass per nearby noncarnivore, count below 16, mate-eligible decisions, births, deaths, juvenile deaths, and cap blocks. The local plant measure is a distance-only proxy; the mating gate also checks route access and same-diet consumers. The litter run peaked at **220 for 10 ticks (1,927–1,936)**; the single run peaked at **184**. There were **zero cap-blocked births** in both, so the emergency cap was reached but did not measurably suppress births in this run. Litter births/deaths totaled **210/227**, juvenile deaths **0**, and final population **49**; single births/deaths totaled **214/198**, juvenile deaths **0**, and final population **82**. These counts include founders and all descendants.

At tick 1,900, litter population was 219, plant biomass 884.9, mean local plant per consumer **3.83**, and **201** sampled consumers were below 16. In the following 100-tick windows, mate-eligible decisions fell from 486 (ending at 1,900) to 16 (ending at 2,300), while deaths exceeded births and population fell to 201. Plant biomass was about 490 at 2,300; last-tick solar regrowth was only 0.406 biomass units. This seed shows delayed resource and mating feedback after growth, with no juvenile mortality as an early density brake. It does **not** establish that the hard cap primarily regulates long-run populations. Earlier Model 1/2 50,000-tick development evidence still shows frequent cap occupancy; that longer-run mechanism remains unresolved.

## Decision and verification

No behavior correction was adopted. Age-only maturation makes the maturity count a weak fitness measure, but the controlled no-food branches already show resource-dependent post-maturity survival and no reproduction. These small interventions do not establish that adding a physiological maturation threshold or juvenile mortality mechanism would improve population ecology. No paired candidate benchmark or holdout was run. Model 0 remains production default; Models 1/2 and unsaved litter v1 remain experimental. Predator scoring and save formats are unchanged. A read-only observer hook now reports plant/carrion biomass consumed for exact focal intake measurement.

Focused scenario tests: 22 passed. Full suite: 99 passed with a 30-second per-test timeout after a concurrent run hit two 5-second timeouts. TypeScript lint and production build passed. No runtime or save replay comparison was needed because simulation behavior did not change. Two consecutive runs of the final diagnostic produced byte-identical JSON (SHA-256 `863034fbae442d71178a095e15dd4413aa38fb27f191e032150d67e880381504`).
