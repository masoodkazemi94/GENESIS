# Juvenile development and reproductive investment experiment

Status: **rejected**, 17 October 2026. Models 0–2 and the Model 0 production default are unchanged. The condition switch and litter v1 are unsaved development probes; neither is adopted. Raw controlled records are in [juvenile_investment_results.json](data/juvenile_investment_results.json), generated by `npx tsx scripts/juvenile_investment_experiment.ts`.

## Life-history finding

`createOrganism` gives offspring `round(34 + 44 × inherited reproductiveInvestment)` reserve, while founders start at 85. Genetically determined `maturityAge` is `round(120 × (0.8 × size + 0.2))`. Ordinary metabolism marks juveniles mature at that age. Juveniles use the same feeding, energy expenditure, health, starvation, and predation rules as adults. Mating separately requires maturity, compatible mate, adequate reserve and hydration, cooldown, age, local food and contact under Model 1. Each parent pays `round(26 + 20 × mean investment)`; litter v1 splits one newborn reserve budget among children, adds six reserve units per parent per extra child, and extends cooldown by 80 ticks per extra child. Senescence begins after the inherited lifespan; starvation and predation can kill before that. The existing age rule is a valid chronological milestone, but the prior audit's nine no-food juveniles reached it without feeding and none reproduced. Thus chronological maturity overstates reproductive readiness in that treatment; it does not establish a missing source-level mortality rule.

## Candidate and controlled design

The single developmental candidate requires genetic minimum age, at least eight units of **realized juvenile food energy gain**, current reserve at least 30, and health at least 70. The intake is accumulated only from successful feeding callbacks, using actual reserve gain; initial parental reserve does not count. These provisional thresholds are attainable at normal age in the abundant, moderate, competition and predator layouts tested here. The gate changes only the maturity transition when explicitly enabled; it does not change feeding, metabolism, hunting, mortality, mating costs, cooldowns or adult behavior. Intake is held in a development-only map keyed by organism ID and removed on death. It is **absent from product saves**. Exact experimental continuation requires explicitly re-enabling the switch and copying that sidecar map with the saved world; this is not production save compatibility.

For each of three reused development seeds (691153, 260679, 10101), a full Model 1 save was cloned into single (58), twin (29), and triplet (20) starting-reserve branches. The focal genome, RNG state, terrain and initial layout matched within branches. Five imposed food/predator layouts ran up to 1,400 ticks. The compatible mate was added after focal maturity. Plants newly reseeded by the engine were removed to preserve the intended food treatment. The natural predator started about 73 units from the focal, outside immediate strike range; detection, chosen pursuit, kills, and escape were recorded. Results are bounded observation outcomes, not lifetime fitness estimates. Censoring is explicit in each offspring record.

Conditions A (ordinary single), B (development with single), and C (existing litter v1) each ran 45 branches. The predeclared gate for D required B to alter first successful reproduction or death time in at least one fed branch, beyond changing the maturity label. B failed; D and the 5,000-tick population screen were **not run**, as required by the staged design. No thresholds were retuned.

The three tested genome maturity ages averaged 123.7 ticks. Every fed B focal reached maturity at exactly its A age; B changed no focal first-reproduction or death tick in those fed branches. At no food intake was zero: B prevented nine maturity labels, including one single still alive and immature at the cutoff, but A also produced zero offspring. Eight no-food focals died in either condition. Five no-food death ticks differed slightly between A and B; the experiment supplies a mate after maturity, so the conditions also differed in later social exposure. Those differences do not establish improved reproductive fitness. In abundant food, A and B each had 21 focal offspring, all reaching maturity by the cutoff and 12 successfully reproducing; C had 34, all mature and 22 reproducing. These numbers reflect different birth counts and the imposed mate/layout, not a demonstrated lifetime litter advantage. In predator branches A/B had 224 focal detections, 115 chosen pursuits and six successful predator feeding events; four focal deaths were recorded. C changed later trajectories and had 655 detections, 80 pursuits, four feeding events, and two focal deaths. These are naturally initiated pursuits, but three genomes and one predator placement per seed cannot isolate a reserve-specific escape effect. The raw file retains individual intake, energy and health at maturity, mortality, first reproduction, descendants, and censored offspring.

Litter v1's prior three-seed 5,000-tick accounting reconciled `parent charges − newborn reserves − overhead = 0`, including extra-child charges and cap-limited partial litters; it produced smaller reserves for twins and triplets and longer parent intervals. This controlled screen independently checks the same transaction for every observed birth event in A, B, and C against the engine's reproduction ledger; all 135 branches had zero accounting discrepancy. The present C branches show extra reproductive descendants in abundant food, but no food-scarcity fitness gain. Partial-litter cap handling remains covered by the existing focused test. The prior screen also touched the cap in one seed and ended with no predators. C is not adopted or presented as rescue. No guaranteed births, threatened-species bonus, juvenile death probability, or added reserve was introduced by B.

## Decision, reproducibility, and roadmap closure

**Reject this developmental candidate.** It correctly separates age from a measurable feeding condition, but it changed no fed focal survival or successful reproduction; no-food branches still had zero reproduction and eight deaths in either condition. Delayed or withheld maturity alone does not justify a population screen. No paired population outcomes, runtime ratio, or broader ecological improvement is claimed.

The development switch repeated exactly on seed 691153 through tick 600, including the intake sidecar; a tick-300 save restored with the sidecar reached the same full saved-state and sidecar hash. The unchanged `determinism_baseline_v2` verifier passed all nine Models 0–2 trajectories through tick 2,000, including repeat and save/restore. Focused maturity and existing reproduction tests passed; the full suite passed **114/114**, TypeScript (`npm run lint`) and production build passed. These checks do not upgrade the unsaved switch to a product save format.

The architecture review's three-task roadmap is now closed: predator encounter placement failed to yield reproducing predator descendants; shared renewable-food feedback passed controlled layouts but caused severe cap interference on seed 166064; this juvenile gate failed its controlled reproductive-fitness test. Litter v1 remains an informative reserve tradeoff, not an adopted mechanic. The next engineering phase should be a **coordinated redesign** of predator income and mate access, renewable resource flow and shared demand, and paid offspring investment with persistent life-history state. Define multi-role lineage and replay gates before implementation; another isolated parameter adjustment on these reused seeds is not supported.
