# Population regulation audit

## Question and method

Does resource limitation regulate population independently of the 220-organism safety cap? This is a bounded observational development audit, not an ecology change or a holdout. Run `npx tsx scripts/population_regulation_audit.ts` to reproduce the nine 10,000-tick runs on existing seeds **691153, 260679, 10101**. Conditions are A: Model 1 single births, B: Model 2 single births, and C: Model 1 with the unsaved litter v1 switch. The [JSON](data/population_regulation_results.json) contains 250-tick checkpoints, ecological roles, resource distribution, birth/death events, reproductive decisions, and cap records. Only A/691153, B/691153, and C/260679 were extended to 30,000 ticks to resolve population rebound versus extinction; their first 10,000 ticks match their shorter paired runs at every checkpoint. Reproduce those extensions with `POP_TICKS=30000 POP_PAIRS=A_model1_single:691153,B_model2_single:691153,C_model1_litter:260679 POP_OUTPUT=/tmp/genesis_population_30k.json npx tsx scripts/population_regulation_audit.ts`.

The real `SimulationEngine` and observer hooks supplied birth, death, feeding, mating eligibility, and cap-block counts. Plant production is the increase in the solar inflow ledger within each window; plant consumption is the observed biomass removed in successful plant feedings. Sampled local food is route-open plant biomass within each noncarnivore's sensory radius divided by nearby competitors (same diet in Model 1, shared noncarnivores in Model 2). Its min, p10, median, p90, max, and mean are stored at every checkpoint. This is an approximation to the mating rule for scavengers because it omits carrion; it is not a direct measurement of assimilated energy. Eligibility counts are organism decisions, not distinct parents or births. Juvenile survival is reported as matured / resolved born offspring; survivors still juvenile at cutoff are censored. The 250-tick samples can miss short resource episodes, while cap occupancy is counted every tick.

Peak is measured each tick; food is checkpoint sampled. All nine runs had at least two distinct episodes with checkpoint median local food below the 16-unit mating threshold. Births and deaths were of similar magnitude, with substantial downturns following early growth. In eight runs, observed plant consumption exceeded solar production. Model 2/260679 is the exception: it went extinct by tick 7,500 even though 10,000-tick aggregate production exceeded consumption. This indicates that total biomass does not guarantee local access or lineage survival.

The ecological roles also changed: no carnivores remained at tick 10,000 in any run. Five final worlds consisted only of scavengers or scavengers plus one to four omnivores; A/260679 and C/260679 retained a few herbivores, B/10101 retained one, and B/260679 had no organisms. Role counts at every checkpoint are in the JSON. Population growth is recorded as both absolute and proportional change for each 250-tick window.

## Feedback and survival

Observed plant depletion, local scarcity, reduced eligibility, fewer births, and deaths form a plausible resource feedback. Across the nine 10,000-tick runs, the median within-run Pearson correlation between end-window local food median and eligibility fraction was **0.885** in the same window and **0.884** one window later. For births per end-window population it was **0.721** and **0.789**; for starvation deaths per population it was **−0.168** and **−0.213**. At two-window lag, the respective medians were **0.746**, **0.623**, and **−0.244**. When correlating *changes* between windows, food change versus eligibility change had median correlation **0.567** in the same window and **0.561** one window later; food change versus birth-rate change had **0.415** and **0.574**. Change correlations with starvation mortality were weak (**−0.054**, **−0.112**). Full ranges and further lags are in the JSON. These associations do not establish causal lag: the mating gate directly uses food, population is in both food and rate denominators, and climate and age structure also change. They do establish that growth and decline occur without cap suppression in eight of nine runs.

Juvenile losses were small: among 3,149 births across the nine runs, **3,113** were observed mature, **10** died before maturity, and **26** remained juvenile at cutoff. This is high observed survival among resolved offspring, consistent with the age-only maturity finding in the juvenile audit. Of **3,378** total deaths, **3,368** were adults at death; this is a death count, not a cohort adult-survival probability. Adult deaths and lost reproductive eligibility are the stronger density brakes here. Deaths included starvation, senescence, thermal stress, and other causes, so density does not explain every decline. For example, A/691153 had 101 starvation, 209 senescence, and 78 thermal deaths by tick 10,000. The source mating rule requires maturity, cooldown, reserves, hydration, and local food; the engine also processes mortality and plant regrowth each tick.

The selected extensions show the limits of recovery. A/691153 fell from a sampled 213 organisms at tick 13,000 to 25 at 17,000, then rose to 137 by 30,000 as plant biomass recovered from a low of 250 and local food improved. B/691153 fell from 215 at tick 15,500 to 13 at 24,750, then recovered to 35 at 30,000; biomass rose from a low of 290 to 9,459 by the final checkpoint. C/260679 fell to **zero at tick 16,000** and remained extinct through 30,000 despite plant biomass returning above 10,000; resource regeneration cannot restore an absent lineage. This is boom-and-bust feedback with occasional recovery, not stable carrying-capacity convergence. Climate and species composition may contribute to each cycle.

## Hard-cap test

The only cap contact in the nine 10,000-tick runs was C/260679: **one 10-tick episode** at 220, with **zero cap-blocked birth attempts**. There was no prolonged saturation. A and B/691153 each peaked at 217 in their 30,000-tick extensions and never hit the cap. C/260679 had no further cap contact by 30,000 and became extinct.

For an isolated 10,000-tick diagnostic, a temporary source copy changed both hard cap checks and the litter slot check from 220 to **260**, and changed only the audit script's cap counter likewise. The process was bounded to 10,000 ticks, a 512 MiB Node heap, a 90-second timeout, and a maximum of 260 organisms. Its peak remained **220**, final population **31**, and births **384**; all 40 core population, food, birth, and death checkpoints matched the 220-cap run. The original cap prevented **zero observed births** in this direct comparison. The temporary copy was discarded; production defaults and save format were untouched. This result applies to this one brief cap-contact trajectory, not the earlier 50,000-tick development runs that reported frequent cap occupancy. No uncapped run was made.

## Decision

**Meaningful resource-dependent population feedback exists.** Plant removal can exceed production, accessible food collapses, the Model 1/2 mating food gates reduce eligibility, and populations decline well below 220 without cap blocking. Resource regeneration sometimes permits renewed births and population recovery. The system also exhibits severe boom-and-bust dynamics, local extinction, and persistent predator failure. This audit found no specific source-level defect that justifies adding a population-control mechanism or changing Models 0–2, scoring, or litter rules. The next engineering priority is controlled predator mate-access and lineage-replacement diagnosis, followed by longer independent-seed validation of any bounded candidate. The three reused seeds and one cap intervention do not establish long-run robustness or explain earlier 50,000-tick cap saturation.
