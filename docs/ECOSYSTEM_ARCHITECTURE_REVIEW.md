# Integrated ecosystem architecture review

Status: design decision, 17 October 2026. No mechanics, default model, save schema, or benchmark data changed in this review. **Model 0 remains production**; Models 1 and 2 are opt-in experiments. The audits below used different code revisions and seed sets; their rates are not pooled.

## Evidence convention

**Confirmed** means a source rule or a completed observation within its stated run. **Hypothesis** means a causal explanation or expected response to a proposed change that has not been demonstrated. **Decision** is a design choice and an experiment to test it, not an implemented feature. Seed 691153, 260679, and 10101 are reused development seeds, not a holdout. Historical 100-seed Model 0 results predate behavior corrections; the later 30- and 20-seed comparisons are development sets.

## Existing ecology: mechanisms and observations

| Area | Confirmed in source and audits | Open inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary production | `ecosystem/ecosystem.ts` starts suitability-weighted plant patches. Growth is limited by patch capacity and responds to light, temperature, moisture, fertility, nutrients, drought, frost, and flood; viable patches are reseeded below a count threshold. Model 0 counts all resources toward that threshold; Models 1/2 count plants and avoid close reseeding. Grazing subtracts biomass; only realized growth/reseeding enters the solar ledger. In eight of nine 10k development runs, plant consumption exceeded measured solar production. [Population regulation](audits/POPULATION_REGULATION_AUDIT.md); [calibration](audits/ECOLOGY_CALIBRATION.md). | Global plant stock is a poor proxy for local edible production and travel cost. The present feedback can recover a depleted habitat, but cannot recover an extinct lineage. |
| Herbivore and scavenger economics | `behavior/behavior.ts` converts a plant bite to reserve at `1.85 × bite × processing × feedingEfficiency` (up to reserve 100). Scavengers can also eat plants; carrion bites use `1.95 × bite × processing × feedingEfficiency`. Carrion comes from non-predation deaths and residual kills and slowly decomposes. Model 0 scavengers prefer any sensed carrion over plants; Models 1/2 score both. [Diagnostics](audits/ECOLOGY_DIAGNOSTICS.md) measured a longer carrion reach reducing completed pursuits in one seed. [Behavior repair](audits/ECOLOGY_BEHAVIOR_REPAIR.md) found Model 1 survivors mostly scavengers at 50k. | Plant fallback and high scavenger processing may blur niches, but their separate causal contribution to dominance is not isolated. Removing fallback could erase scavengers when predators fail. |
| Predator transfer | Predators spend size/speed/sensing dependent maintenance and velocity-dependent movement, including a pursuit multiplier; rest discounts costs. `behavior/foraging.ts` rejects blocked, too-fast, or negative expected-return prey in Models 1/2. Contact strikes damage prey; reserve transfer occurs only on a lethal strike and is limited by prey reserve/body size and predator headroom. Residual carcass becomes carrion. Across three Model 1 development seeds at 5k, food gain was 3,384 versus 5,353 spent, with no births. [Predator viability](audits/PREDATOR_VIABILITY.md). In 16 cloned rejected-prey branches, forced pursuit improved incremental net energy in four; both rejection groups were negative in aggregate. [Scoring validation](audits/PREDATOR_SCORING_VALIDATION.md). | The deficit may reflect encounter geometry, capture frequency, prey composition, and movement costs together. The forced-pursuit result argues against simply relaxing rejection gates or raising transfer as a proven fix. |
| Mating and parental investment | `behavior/behavior.ts` requires maturity, cooldown, reserve, hydration, age, compatible genome, a sensed partner, and winning utility. Models 1/2 also require route access and local food per consumer, with a high-reserve carnivore exception. Contact charges each parent `round(26 + 20 × mean investment)`; a normal newborn starts at `round(34 + 44 × child investment)` reserve. The engine now books actual overhead and refunds blocked queued births. Experimental litter v1 splits one newborn reserve budget among 1–3 offspring and adds parental charges/cooldown; it is unsaved and unadopted. [Predator energy](audits/PREDATOR_ENERGY_AUDIT.md); [reproductive strategy](audits/REPRODUCTIVE_STRATEGY_EXPERIMENT.md). | A wider mate radius alone did not rescue predator lineages. Extra newborns are not evidence of greater lifetime reproductive fitness. |
| Juveniles and lifespan | `organism/organism.ts` matures offspring at `round(120 × (0.8 × size + 0.2))` ticks solely by age. Juveniles retain adult body/feeding/metabolic rules; no growth or juvenile reserve condition exists. Lifespan is a seeded 2,400–4,200 tick draw scaled by endurance, followed by increasing senescence. In 45 cloned Model 1 trials on three genomes, all 36 without immediate predator contact matured, including nine without plants; nine exposed at immediate contact died on tick 1. No-food branches produced no offspring and lower birth reserves led to earlier *post-maturity* death. [Juvenile survival](audits/JUVENILE_SURVIVAL_AUDIT.md). | Age-only maturation makes mature-offspring counts an inadequate fitness outcome; a different juvenile mechanism might improve selection, but has not been tested. |
| Density and space | Founders are placed on valid land, with 42 herbivores, 15 scavengers, and nine carnivores (`generation/worldGenerator.ts`); habitat suitability shapes plants, and terrain/water restrict routes. The engine uses spatial hash queries and a 220-organism safety ceiling. Models 1/2 add local standing-food mating gates; Model 2 shares plant competition across noncarnivores. The nine 10k regulation runs had no blocked births and showed scarcity, reproductive suppression, crashes, and sometimes recovery. The separate 20-seed 50k Model 2 screen still spent 19,252 ticks at cap, versus 36,640 for Model 1. [Population regulation](audits/POPULATION_REGULATION_AUDIT.md); [performance](audits/SIMULATION_PERFORMANCE_AUDIT.md). | The short-run resource feedback is real; the long-run cap mechanism and whether patch *production* can support nearby consumers remain unsettled. A stable equilibrium is not required. |
| Evolution and speciation | `genetics/genetics.ts` recombines bounded heritable traits with undirected mutation; survival and mating filter them. Diet affinity can change diet. Cross-taxon compatibility rejects distance above 0.28; same-taxon mating tolerates up to 0.35. `engine.ts` creates a new taxon when **one newborn** exceeds 0.32 from the founder baseline and is isolated from that baseline. The ten corrected diagnostic worlds had maximum measured child-founder distance 0.120; historical 100-seed 100k runs and a selected 500k survivor produced no new species. [Diagnostics](audits/ECOLOGY_DIAGNOSTICS.md); [baseline](audits/SIMULATION_AUDIT.md). | Trait change is possible, but a newborn threshold does not demonstrate persistent population separation or reproductive isolation. No threshold reduction is justified by current evidence. |
| Compute and persistence | `engine.ts` steps a seeded PRNG, serializes its state, ecology config, organisms, resources, terrain deltas, and history; saves are validated/recovered in `persistence/`, with one writer tab coordinated separately. Model 1 food scoring and route checks dominate measured headless CPU under load. After optimization, six Model 0/1 saved-state hashes matched, and 20-seed Model 1 50k runtime fell from 843.977 to 758.567 seconds; no Model 0 speed change was demonstrated. [Performance](audits/SIMULATION_PERFORMANCE_AUDIT.md). | Browser frame time and replay of a future behavior candidate require direct verification. New persistent fields or changed RNG draw order can alter old-world continuation. |

## Architectural diagnosis and design decisions

**Decision:** Keep the current engine, spatial grid, resources, and inheritance model as the base. The failures are coupled assumptions rather than one confirmed broken coefficient. Plant biomass is shared by herbivores and plant-eating scavengers, but Model 1's mating denominator divides only by the same diet; Model 2 fixes that denominator but still gates on standing stock rather than replenishment and can remain cap-limited. Predator reproduction needs *both* positive realized hunt income and a compatible reachable mate in the same viable period. Natural Model 1 had only 10 reachable-mate observations in 585 predator samples; doubling mate range increased this to 23/603 without births, while founder clustering raised access but reduced food and ended with no predators. [Viability](audits/PREDATOR_VIABILITY.md). More food, wider sensing, extra births, or a relaxed hunt score in isolation is therefore an unsupported solution.

**Resource design:** Treat patch growth as the renewable flow and grazing as its depletion; measure local reachable flow, consumption, and travel expenditure over a reproductive interval. Preserve climate-driven variation and depletion. The 220 ceiling remains a safety guard, while births and deaths should normally be governed by finite intake, parental expenditure, offspring survival, and competition. For scavengers, retain both carrion and plant fallback initially, but represent shared plant demand consistently and test a feeding tradeoff only if dominance persists. Any trait cost/benefit changes must be paired against prey, scavenger, and predator outcomes; never add reserve by an untracked rescue rule.

**Predator design:** First use the existing habitat and founder placement system to test co-location of predators, reachable prey, and compatible conspecifics, without adding organisms or changing prey scoring. This tests whether early encounter geometry can jointly improve hunt income and mate opportunities. If it fails, reject placement as the answer and only then test a bounded capture/feeding mechanism using observed net episode returns; forced pursuit evidence rules out indiscriminate chase expansion. Prey must retain escape, depletion, and local refuges so predator gains do not require prey collapse. Carnivore success means descendants that themselves reproduce, not founder survival or a single birth.

**Life history and evolution design:** Keep reproduction paid from parental reserves. A later candidate should make offspring count depend on reachable food flow and parental surplus, split a fixed newborn budget, and charge an explicit interval cost; scarce food should favor fewer, better endowed offspring without an emergency bonus. Juvenile development should depend on accumulated feeding/body condition as well as age, with real competition, predation, and mortality; assess adult offspring that themselves reproduce. Existing heritable speed, size, sensing, efficiency, tolerance, investment, and diet provide selection axes, but their benefits must carry measured travel/maintenance and birth costs. Spatially distinct habitats and limited dispersal can create persistent mating neighborhoods. Define speciation only after a *population* cluster shows sustained trait divergence, within-cluster mating, and reduced between-cluster compatible mating for multiple generations. Do not force isolation or declare a species from a single outlier newborn. This is a later stage after lineages can replace themselves; no speciation mechanism is approved for implementation now.

## Ranked implementation roadmap

Priority reflects ecological impact / evidence / complexity / save and replay risk / CPU cost / spectator value. The rankings are judgments, not measured outcomes. Each candidate stays isolated from Model 0 and saved production worlds until it passes the scorecard; an ephemeral development switch is acceptable for a screen, but not a product save format.

| Rank and task | Why this order and costs | Smallest useful experiment and decision gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Predator encounter placement candidate** — in `generation/worldGenerator.ts` and terrain spawn helpers, reposition the existing nine predator founders in walkable regions that have reachable prey *and* at least one compatible predator within mating range. Retain the same animal counts, genomes, and resources. Use a separate deterministic placement stream after ordinary generation so the candidate does not alter later genomes through generation draw order. | **High** possible impact; **moderate** evidence (measured income deficit and mate scarcity); **low–moderate** code complexity; **medium** RNG/old-save risk if enabled outside a switch; **low** per-tick CPU, one-time generation work; **high** spectator value from possible hunts and descendants. Earlier clustering alone reduced food, so joint overlap is the test, not an assumed fix. | Compare existing Model 1 and a generation-only development switch on **691153, 260679, 10101**, 5k ticks, with matched initial genomes/resources and no extra entities. Record reachable prey/mates, realized net hunt energy, births, offspring reaching adulthood, generation-3+ births, prey/scavenger survival, cap ticks, and runtime. **Advance** only if at least two seeds show both improved predator net intake and later-generation reproduction without worse prey/role collapse or cap dependence; otherwise reject placement and move to a bounded capture-return design. Serialize at the first birth and after restore; compare full state and PRNG to uninterrupted continuation. Default and old-save trajectories must remain unchanged. |
| **2. Shared renewable-food feedback candidate** — in `behavior/behavior.ts`, `ecosystem/ecosystem.ts`, and spatial queries, replace the experimental noncarnivore mating gate's standing-patch amount per diet with a cheap route-reachable estimate of plant *replenishment plus available stock* over the parental interval, divided among all nearby plant eaters; count carrion separately for scavengers. Keep the hard cap. | **High** impact on herbivore/scavenger coexistence and cap use; **moderate–strong** evidence for shared competition and scarcity, **moderate** implementation complexity; **medium** save/replay risk from decision changes; **medium** CPU risk if route checks multiply; **high** spectator value from more visible cycles and roles. Formula and thresholds are hypotheses to calibrate, not established values. | Reuse the three 10k regulation seeds in paired development runs, plus the cap-prone seed **166064** for a focused 10k check. Record local realized intake/production, eligibility, birth-to-reproductive-adult replacement, role shares, cap ticks/blocked births, and extinction. **Advance** if cap interference falls in the cap-prone case without replacing it with systematic collapse or worsening multi-role persistence across paired seeds; otherwise reject. Benchmark per-organism tick cost at dense load and require a predeclared acceptable bound. Version any adopted behavior; old saves keep their model and replay hashes. |
| **3. Juvenile and reproductive-investment candidate** — in `organism/organism.ts`, `engine.ts`, and `behavior/behavior.ts`, test condition-dependent maturity and a resource-dependent 1–3-child budget that divides newborn reserve and extends parental interval. Require a minimum age *and* measured feeding/body condition for maturity; do not grant reserve or mortality protection. | **Medium–high** impact on density regulation and selection; **strong** evidence that age-only maturity is weak and litters increased counts without fitness proof; **moderate–high** complexity; **high** save compatibility risk if condition state is persisted; **low–medium** CPU cost; **medium–high** spectator value through meaningful generations. Exact growth and litter rules remain candidate choices. | First repeat the cloned high/moderate/no-food, competition, and predator exposures from [juvenile survival](audits/JUVENILE_SURVIVAL_AUDIT.md), then paired 5k development seeds **691153, 260679, 10101** against single births. **Advance** only if food/reserve materially changes maturation and birth-to-reproductive-adult replacement, without an artificial juvenile death spike, worse predator-lineage loss, or sustained cap blocking. Count censored cohorts separately. A development-only switch does not qualify for adoption: any saved condition variables need validation, old-save defaults, and exact post-birth/post-maturity replay. |

After these bounded tasks, use the same scorecard on an independent seed panel before considering promotion. If predator placement fails, the next predator candidate is limited capture/feeding economics, not a broader rejected-prey chase. Population-level speciation follows only if geographically distinct, reproducing lineages persist long enough to test isolation. No Model 3, production promotion, parameter value, or new long-horizon benchmark is authorized by this document.

## Ecological scorecard and acceptance rules

Use paired seeds, fixed tick horizons, per-seed trajectories, and explicit censoring. Report counts *and* fractions with denominators; keep founder, generation-2, and generation-3+ outcomes separate. A score at one endpoint cannot substitute for time series or causation. Initial screens above decide whether a candidate merits an independent panel; they do not prove general viability.

| Measure | Definition and interpretation | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Worlds non-extinct; herbivore, scavenger, carnivore persistence | Fraction alive by role at each checkpoint and extinction times; include complete extinction and recovery where possible. | **Descriptive outcomes**; avoid a fixed equilibrium or 100% survival target. A candidate that systematically erases a role fails ecological review. |
| Carnivore multiple generations; birth-to-adult reproductive replacement | By role and cohort: born → mature → first successful reproduction → reproducing descendants; report right-censored cohorts and generation-3+ births. | **Hard evidence gate** for claiming predator viability or a fitness benefit; maturity alone is insufficient. |
| Role diversity and dominance | Number of roles, each role's share, dominant-role share, and duration of one-role phases; do not count a token minority as robust coexistence. | **Descriptive**, with regressions considered in adoption. |
| Cap interference | Ticks at 220, blocked birth attempts, cap occupancy duration, and matched bounded cap comparison where needed. | **Hard gate**: no candidate may claim natural regulation when births are substantially cap-blocked; emergency contact is reported, not automatically failure. |
| Resources and population dynamics | Local reachable plant/carrion stock and production, consumption, realized energy gain/spend, reproduction eligibility, cohort deaths, boom/bust amplitude, and habitat distribution. | **Diagnostic/descriptive**; cycles and local extinction are allowed. |
| Genetic divergence and actual speciation | Within/between-population distances, spatial separation, compatible cross-cluster encounters, multi-generation within-cluster reproduction, and new taxon persistence. Distinguish source threshold firings from population isolation. | **Hard evidence gate** for a speciation claim; zero events alone is descriptive until a suitable mechanism and horizon exist. |
| Performance | Headless mean/p95 tick, per-organism cost, behavior/route-query counts, save size, and browser responsiveness before adoption. Compare at matched population loads. | **Hard engineering gate**: predeclare an acceptable budget and reject an unaffordable candidate. Existing profile is not a browser measurement. |
| Deterministic replay and saves | Same-seed repeated trajectories; save/restore state, PRNG, event/ID sequence and next checkpoints; old-save Model 0 behavior and validation/recovery. | **Hard invariant**: exact replay and safe old saves. A development-only unsaved switch is evidence for a concept, not promotion. |

Historical calibration's numerical targets were provisional and unmet; this review does not turn them into new success thresholds. Any claimed ecological improvement must show paired, multi-role and lineage evidence, acceptable runtime, and replay, followed by independent seeds before a production decision.
